# Test Day: Booking and Logistics

Do this early, not in the last week. Booking the test gives you a real date, and the whole plan
works best with one.

## Book the test (this week)

- [ ] Find the official booking page for your test (the exam board, certification body, school, or test center).
- [ ] While you book, check the name and ID rules. The name on the booking has to match your ID exactly. These rules change, so check the official page, not someone's summary.
- [ ] Choose how you'll take it (in person, online with a proctor, or in class). In person, less can go wrong on the day. Online, you don't travel, but the room and connection checks are strict.
- [ ] Book a date on or near your target. If it's different from the date in the map, update `learner-map.md` → Deadline the same day.
- [ ] Read the cancellation and reschedule policy while you're booking.
- [ ] If you need accommodations, ask early, because they take time and paperwork. Write them in `learner-map.md` → Accessibility, and practise with the same setup (extra time, assistive tools) before the day.

## Check the syllabus again (last month)

- [ ] **30 days before:** have your AI tutor check the official syllabus, course outline, or exam guide again for your test date. Update the source version, effective date, checked date, and status in `learner-map.md`. If the content changed, update the affected topics, practice tasks, and questions. If the format changed (question types, number of questions, time, weightings), update "How I'm tested" in the map and make your last practice tests match. If it can't confirm the current version, give it the latest official material before you make any more exam practice.
- [ ] **7 days before:** do the same check again, update the same fields, and update your study material if anything changed.
- [ ] Check sooner if your test date, exam code or version, or jurisdiction changes, or if the exam provider announces a change.

## Before the test

- In the last 6 days, take two full practice tests in the real format (same question types, number of questions, and timing), and run the Diagnostician on every miss.
- 2 days before: light review only, from your own cheat sheets and due flashcards. No new cards and no new topics.
- On the morning of the test: at most 30 minutes of cheat sheets, then stop. Don't study in the last 2 hours before the test.

## On the day

- Check the ID rules on the official page again. They change, and lists on other sites get out of date.
- Online with a proctor: use a room with the door closed and a clear desk, with no headphones and no second screen, on a stable connection. The proctor will check your room. Log in about 30 minutes early for check-in.
- In person: get there 15 to 30 minutes early.
- Don't bring anything you don't need. Notes and phones stay outside.

## During the test

This is what you practised in Sparring:

- Pace yourself: total minutes divided by the number of questions, from your Intake.
- Read each question twice. Look for words like *best*, *most likely*, *least*, *without*, and *only*.
- Rule out 2 options first. Flag the ones you're unsure about and move on, but always answer if wrong answers don't cost points.
- The ways you usually slip up are in your Intake. Don't switch a right answer to a wrong one just because of a hunch.

## After

- [ ] Write your result and score in `daily-tracker.md` → After action, with your weakest and strongest area and the root cause that mattered most.
- [ ] If you passed, close the goal in the learner map.

## If you need a retake

You don't need a new plan. Your mistake log and root causes already are one.

- [ ] Run the Diagnostician (prompt §8) on your score report (if you got one) and your mistake log. Write the root causes into the map, and put their fix drills first. Then run the Mapmaker (prompt §2) again around them.
- [ ] Check the official retake rules: waiting period, how many attempts you get, and fees. Check the official page, not someone's summary.
- [ ] Book the retake and put the new date in `learner-map.md` → Deadline the same day. That sets up the 30-day and 7-day syllabus checks again for the new date.
- [ ] Have your tutor check the official requirements for the new date. The exam version can change between attempts, because exams get retired or replaced. Update the source status, and update anything that's out of date if the version changed.
- [ ] Keep your levels, because what you learned is still real. Keep doing your due reviews so the topics you worked hard on don't fade while you wait.
